Source notes

- Coordinates from OpenStreetMap Nominatim unless stated (https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org), cross-checked against luzern.com for the palace.

Headline findings
1. **Dance of Death: the brief is right, with a gap.** A Dance of Death cycle (Jakob von Wyl, c. 1615) really is in the Ritter palace (Regierungsgebäude, former Jesuit college). Lucerne has a second, far better known cycle on the Spreuerbrücke (Kaspar Meglinger, 1626-37). The brief does not mention the Spreuerbrücke; the site should not let guests confuse the two. See ritter-palace.
2. **Suidter pharmacy: 1833 CONFIRMED by the pharmacy itself.** Founder Dr. Leopold Suidter. Address Bahnhofstrasse 21, 6003 Luzern, not near Hirschenplatz. Ownership changed in 2011 and 2023 (owner died 2023); the shop still trades in 2026.
3. **Bridges and tunnels on Susten:** the brief says Switzerland Tourism notes a "remarkable number". Counts differ by source (11, 24 or 26 tunnels). The story avoids a number.
4. **Hotel Belvédère (Furka):** closed since 2015. The brief's "bends around Hotel Belvédère" is fine, but the hotel is not open to guests.
5. **Season today (2026-10-15):** all three passes are open, Furka with a night closure, and a listed closing date of 27 October 2026. Snow can close them earlier.

## ritter-palace — Ritter'scher Palast (Rittersche Palast), Lucerne
Used in tours: 3 (The city's quieter lives)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Italian Renaissance-inspired palazzo in the middle of Lucerne" — CONFIRMED — Florentine-style Renaissance palazzo, called the city's most impressive Renaissance palazzo. — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/ritterscher-palace
- "Built in the sixteenth century" — CONFIRMED — Construction from 1556 (inventory says 1556-61, plans by Domenico Solbiolo, executed by Giovanni Pietro del Grilio); Lux Ritter died in 1559, so the city finished the building (1573-74 under Ulrich Root per the inventory). Tourism pages instead name an architect "Giovanni Lynzo" who was sentenced to death for heresy; the inventory names other builders, so the architect is UNVERIFIED and left out of the story. — https://api3.geo.admin.ch/featureattachments/ch.babs.kulturgueter/PDF/kgs_03737_gsk-d.pdf (read via search-result summary, direct fetch returned 404); https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/articles/014473
- "Expresses the ambition of elites" (status) — CONFIRMED as description of the builder's motive: "commissioned the palace to show his social standing". Ritter is called the richest and most powerful Lucerner of his time. — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/ritterscher-palace ; https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/articles/014473
- "Later housed Jesuits" — CONFIRMED — handed to the Jesuits as a residence in 1577 (one Lucerne Tourism text says 1557; the inventory and luzern.com say 1577, used here). Jesuit college dissolved 1773. — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/ritterscher-palace
- "Today serves the cantonal government" — CONFIRMED — government building since 1804. — same luzern.com page
- "Its most thought-provoking theme is the Dance of Death ... a historic corridor" — CONFIRMED, with a correction of emphasis. The palace does hold a Dance of Death: "seven pictures, 23 scenes", Jakob von Wyl, c. 1615, in a corridor (floor given as top, third or second floor in different sources). The HLS says "zugeschrieben" (attributed): "Totentanzzyklus im Luzerner Jesuitenkollegium (heute Regierungsgebäude)", 1610-1615. — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/rittersche-palast ; https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/019169
- Correction to avoid confusion: Lucerne's famous Totentanz is the one on the **Spreuerbrücke**, by Kaspar Meglinger, 1626-1637, originally 72 panels (about 45 survive). Two separate cycles, both in Lucerne. — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/019096 (72 panels, 1626-37); panel count 45: https://rheinland.museum-digital.de/pdf/object/20378.pdf?lang=de (museum record found via search, not opened)
- "The courtyard may be visited on working days" — CONFIRMED — inner courtyard and Dance of Death open Monday to Friday in office hours per luzern.com (no times given; no canton page found). — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/ritterscher-palace

### Sourced facts
- Lux Ritter (died 10 May 1559): saddler, wine, cloth and fish trader, moneylender; Schultheiss (chief magistrate) 1556 and 1558; French captain, later colonel; French pension distributor 1556-59; "richest and most powerful Lucerner of his time". — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/articles/014473
- Palace is the Regierungsgebäude, Bahnhofstrasse 15, 6002/6003 Luzern; listed as cantonal heritage since 1963, restored 1984-86. — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/rittersche-palast ; https://www.HMdb.org/m.asp?m=67733 (via search summary; this page lists "Bahnhofstrasse 17", the luzern.com address is used)
- The Jesuits added a west wing in 1695 and an east wing toward the church in 1756-57. — https://www.HMdb.org/m.asp?m=67733 (search summary only, single source)
- Semicircular Grand Council chamber in neoclassical style by Melchior Berri, 1841-43. — https://www.HMdb.org/m.asp?m=67733 (search summary only)
- Building is attached directly to the Jesuit Church. — luzern.com search summary of palace page; OSM shows the palace on Bahnhofstrasse beside the church.
- Barrier-free: luzern.com flags the building as wheelchair accessible with accessible toilets. — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/rittersche-palast

### Story
EN title: A Florentine palace on Bahnhofstrasse
EN body: Lux Ritter, a Lucerne magistrate and captain in French service, had this palace built from about 1556. He chose the Florentine Renaissance style, with arcades around a courtyard. He died in 1559, before it was finished. In 1577 the city gave the house to the Jesuits, and since 1804 it has been the seat of the cantonal government. On an upper floor, a corridor holds a Dance of Death, painted around 1615 and attributed to Jakob von Wyl. In seven pictures, Death leads people of every rank away. The semicircular parliament chamber was added in 1841.

## suidter-pharmacy — Alte Suidtersche Apotheke, Lucerne
Used in tours: 3 (The city's quieter lives)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "The historic Suidter pharmacy, founded in 1833" — CONFIRMED — the pharmacy's own history page: opened 1833 by Dr. med. Leopold Suidter (1803-1855). — https://www.suidter.ch/geschichte
- "A place to talk about everyday Lucerne: illness, advice, a cup of herbal tea" — UNVERIFIED — no source found for herbal tea being served or sold; the pharmacy describes itself as a naturopathic and conventional pharmacy. Left out of the story. — https://www.suidter.ch/
- "Wooden cabinetry, bottles and the language of the pharmacist" — PARTLY CONFIRMED — a 2017 trade-press report says the interior is "praktisch noch aus der Anfangszeit", and mentions a display window with an old pharmacy workshop and standing vessels, a prescription book from before 1540 and some herbal preparations from the founding era. Single trade source, 2017, under the previous owner: current state UNVERIFIED. — https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/internationales/schweiz-von-der-drogeriekette-in-die-kraeuter-apotheke/
- "It remains a working business" — CONFIRMED — Open Monday-Friday 08:00-12:15 and 13:30-18:30, Saturday 08:00-16:00, Sunday closed; website footer dated 2026. — https://www.suidter.ch/
- "Claims about special recipes or numbers of herbs should be confirmed with the shop" — AGREED. The 2017 report says about 350 herbs, a 2023 obituary-type report says over 300; the numbers conflict and are not used. — https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/internationales/schweiz-von-der-drogeriekette-in-die-kraeuter-apotheke/ ; https://www.zentralplus.ch/news/inhaber-der-alten-suidterschen-apotheke-verstorben-2527351/ (snippet only; page blocked)

- "Allow 10-20 minutes and ask before indoor photography" — keep as advice; no source needed.

### Sourced facts
- Leopold Suidter, born November 1803 in Stein (Aargau), studied medicine in Vienna, returned to Switzerland 1827, took over the "Cloos-Haus" in summer 1832 and opened the pharmacy in 1833. — https://www.suidter.ch/geschichte
- Owners: Otto Suidter senior (1833-1901) from 1855; Otto Suidter junior (1866-1936) from 1901, who rebuilt it in 1906; René Rickli 1937; Walter Büttiker 1956; Dr. Bibiana Longauer 1980 (died 2010); Dr. Matteo Schaffhauser 2011; since 2023 Franziska Tovo Schaffhauser, managed by Claudia Weingartner. — https://www.suidter.ch/geschichte
- The pharmacy calls itself "Älteste Apotheke in Luzern"; a chronicle by Aldo Colombi (c. 2000) is titled "Suidter'sche Apotheke: älteste Apotheke in Luzern seit 1833". Use "calls itself", not "is". — https://www.suidter.ch/ ; https://wellcomecollection.org/works/ejk5ky59
- Matteo Schaffhauser died in early 2023 at 63; the building was later reported sold to a new owner family (zentralplus, snippet only; the pharmacy continues to operate per its 2026 website). — https://www.zentralplus.ch/news/inhaber-der-alten-suidterschen-apotheke-verstorben-2527351/

## furka — Furka Pass
Used in tours: Alpine journey "Furka"

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Furka Pass links Uri with the upper Rhône valley" — CONFIRMED — links Realp (Uri) with the Goms (Upper Valais); summit 2,429 m. — https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-gb/experiences/summer-autumn/bicycle-mountain-bike/road-cycling/the-most-beautiful-mountain-passes-in-switzerland/ ; https://alpen-paesse.ch/en/alpenpaesse/furkapass/
- "Its carriage road opened in 1867" — CONFIRMED, with nuance — Uri and Valais built the road Oberwald-Hospental in 1863-1866, with the Confederation paying two-thirds for military reasons; postal coaches ran from 1867 to 1920. MySwitzerland says "open since 1867". — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008807/ ; MySwitzerland page above
- "Bends around Hotel Belvédère so famous" — CONFIRMED (the hotel sits on a tight bend at about 2,270 m) with a correction on status: the Belvédère has been closed since 2015, so it is a landmark to look at. — https://www.swiss-spectator.ch/?p=37666 (secondary; the hotel's own closure year only on this source and freizeit.ch). Height 2,272 m: https://switzerlanding.com/james-bond-switzerland/
- "The Rhône Glacier is a changing character ... reveals its retreat" — CONFIRMED — tongue at about 1,760 m close to the Gletsch hotels in 1874; about 2,240 m in 2007; retreat of about 2 km between 1874 and 2014. Still opposite the Belvédère in 1980; a lake lies in between by 2022. — https://diercke.de/node/2873 ; https://www.swiss-spectator.ch/?p=37666
- "In 1964 Furka appeared in the James Bond film Goldfinger" — CONFIRMED — MySwitzerland: "setting for a James Bond car chase in Goldfinger". Details of the scenes vary (e.g. Tilly Masterson's car) and are not used. — MySwitzerland page above; https://switzerlanding.com/james-bond-switzerland/
- "Carry warm layers; the pass closes seasonally" — CONFIRMED — see 2026 access. — https://alpen-paesse.ch/en/alpenpaesse/furkapass/

### Sourced facts
- Length 29 km (12 km from Realp, 17 km from Oberwald), summit 2,429 m. — https://alpen-paesse.ch/en/alpenpaesse/furkapass/
- First inn in Gletsch 1830; 600 hotel beds between Gletsch and Realp by 1930. — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008807/
- Furka base railway tunnel (1982) took year-round traffic off the pass. — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008807/
- Belvédère sold by the canton to Philipp Carlen in 1988, reopened 1990, closed 2015. The ice grotto in the Rhône Glacier was first dug in 1870 and is re-cut every spring. — https://www.swiss-spectator.ch/?p=37666 (single secondary source) ; https://diercke.de/node/2873 (grotto about 120 years, 100 m)
- Hotel Furkablick on the Uri side is the only hotel still operating on the pass, as a gallery (per the same Swiss Spectator page, written some years ago). Status UNVERIFIED for 2026.

### Sourced facts
- Built 1938-45 (some sources 1939-46), opened 7 September 1946; on opening day about 15,000 vehicles crossed, about one eighth of all vehicles registered in Switzerland. — https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/157833/ ; https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008862
- Cost 31.8 million francs. — https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.astra.ivs-nat/PDF/UR00040400.pdf
- Highest point 2,224 m; max gradient 9 per cent; 6 m wide. Passhöhe tunnel 325 m long, 70 m of cover, with a coat-of-arms plaque at the middle marking the Bern-Uri border. — same ASTRA inventory
- All concrete parts clad in natural stone (gneiss and granite from the excavation). — same ASTRA inventory
- Workers: soldiers, convicts and interned Russians and Poles; mostly shovels, picks and wheelbarrows; the "Polencher" curve at Obermatt was built by Polish internees. Brief's engineer: Dr. Walter Bösiger led the Bernese side. — https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/157833/ ; https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/193513/
- Steingletscher: about 4.3 km long, starts on the ridge between Gwächtenhorn (3,404 m) and Sustenhorn (3,502 m); about 1 km longer at the mid-19th-century peak; the Steinsee formed in the old tongue basin from 1940 onward, at 1,934 m. — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steingletscher (pointer)
- Steinalp discovery path from the Berghotel Steingletscher to the glacier, about 2 h return; hotel listed open June-October. — https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ch/accommodations/berghotel-steingletscher/ (search summary)
- Viewpoint on the Himmelrank hairpin at 1,946 m on the west ascent, looking toward Gadmen. — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustenpass (pointer)

### Story
EN title: Susten, the road built for cars
EN body: The Susten was the first new alpine road in Switzerland planned for motor cars. Built from 1938 and opened on 7 September 1946, it climbs to 2,224 m with a gradient of no more than 9 per cent.
