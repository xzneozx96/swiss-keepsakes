## gotthard — Gotthard Pass (Schöllenen Gorge, Devil's Bridge, Tremola, summit)
Used in tours: Alpine journey "Gotthard" (one story, one journey plan)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Crossing the Schöllenen Gorge and the area of the Devil's Bridge challenged generations of bridge builders, guides and merchants" — CONFIRMED — the gorge was opened as a route between 1150 and 1230 by a bridge over the Reuss; the builders and commissioners are unknown (HLS suspects the Walser of Ursern). — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796
- "The legend that the devil helped build the bridge is not engineering history" — CONFIRMED — HLS only says the legend was attached to the structure later; the name "Teiffels Brucken" is first written in 1587 (earlier name "stiebende Brugge", 1306). — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796
- "The cobbled bends of the Tremola preserve a sense of slower travel" — CONFIRMED with detail — road Göschenen-Airolo built 1827-1830 to plans of Ticino engineer Francesco Meschini (ticino.ch); other sources give 1827-1832. About 4 km, about 300 m climb, 24 hairpins (ticino.ch); one Lucerne tour page says 37 bends, treat the count as source-dependent. What you see today is largely the reconstruction finished in 1951; much of the granite paving and the kilometre stones survive. — https://www.ticino.ch/de/commons/details/Tremolastrasse-/137877.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremola_San_Gottardo (pointer only)
- "Later, railways, tunnels and modern roads transformed Alpine crossings" — CONFIRMED — Gotthard rail tunnel 1882 (about 15 km), road tunnel 5 Sept 1980 (16.9 km), Base Tunnel 2016 (57 km). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Road_Tunnel ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass ; HLS notes the gorge stayed important despite the 1882 railway. — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796
- "Allow a full day if combining Devil's Bridge, Tremola and the summit" — CONFIRMED as realistic — see journey plan (about 6.5 h, ESTIMATE).
- Not in the brief but a common error: the Devil's Bridge people walk over today dates from 1830 (HLS: Karl Emanuel Müller, 1828-1830); the road bridge beside it is later (HLS: 1955-1956; Wikipedia gorge article says 1958). Dates for the 1830 bridge agree across sources; do not print a date for the road bridge.

### Sourced facts
- Gorge opened as a route between 1150 and 1230; bridge called "stiebende Brugge" 1306, "Teiffels Brucken" 1587; builders unknown — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796
- 1828-1830 Karl Emanuel Müller built a carriage road and a new Devil's Bridge — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796
- Pietro Morettini cut the Urnerloch in 1707-1708, "the first road tunnel of the Alps" — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796
- 1799: Russian and French troops fought in the gorge; Suvorov monument built 1895-1898 (HLS), 1898 (newlyswissed) — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008796 ; https://www.newlyswissed.com/devils-bridge-in-goschenen/
- Legend: villagers promise the devil the first soul across, send a goat; the devil hurls a boulder (the Teufelsstein near Göschenen). Meinrad Lienert's 1915 retelling; Scheuchzer's earlier version uses a dog. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teufelsbr%C3%BCcke (pointer) ; https://www.newlyswissed.com/devils-bridge-in-goschenen/
- Teufelsbild painted on the rock by Heinrich Danioth, 1950; Suvorov monument two minutes from the car park; about 30 min circular walk from Restaurant Teufelsbrücke over the 1830 bridge; path can be wet; site listed "Nicht barrierefrei"; season May to October — https://www.myswitzerland.com/de/erlebnisse/schoellenenschlucht-und-teufelsbruecke/
- Pass summit 2,106 m (alpen-paesse, Wikipedia); Hospental to Airolo 24 km, max gradient 9%; Tremola weight limit 3.5 t — https://www.alpen-paesse.ch/en/alpenpaesse/gotthardpass/
- Tremola: "longest road-building monument in Switzerland", 6-7 m wide, walls up to 8 m; both road keepers' houses gone (one destroyed 1989, one by avalanche 1874) — https://www.ticino.ch/de/commons/details/Tremolastrasse-/137877.html
- Fort Hospiz built 1893-1894 as part of the Gotthard fortifications (military history, not a pilgrim hospice story to promise) — https://www.csi-alps.unibe.ch/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/RGo-E1-E2-E3-E4-Seedorf-Wassen-Gotthardpass.pdf
- National Gotthard Museum reopened June 2022 after two years of building work, seven thematic rooms (opening dates for 2026 not found) — https://maps.adac.de/poi/museo-nationale-del-san-gottardo-san-gottardo
- Road tunnel: opened 5 Sept 1980, 16.9 km, single bore; second tube planned for 2029-2032 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Road_Tunnel

### Story
EN title: From the Devil's Bridge to the Tremola
EN body: North of Andermatt the Reuss runs through the Schöllenen Gorge. Between about 1150 and 1230 people found a way through it and built a bridge, the Devil's Bridge. Nobody knows who built it. Legend says the devil did, and that the villagers cheated him by sending a goat across first. The stone bridge you can walk over today dates from 1830. On the south side the Tremola climbs to the pass in 24 hairpin bends on granite cobbles, finished around 1830. The railway tunnel opened in 1882, the road tunnel in 1980. The pass road itself closes for the winter.
DE title: Von der Teufelsbrücke zur Tremola
DE body: Nördlich von Andermatt fliesst die Reuss durch die Schöllenenschlucht. Zwischen etwa 1150 und 1230 fand man einen Weg hindurch und baute eine Brücke, die Teufelsbrücke. Wer sie gebaut hat, weiss niemand. Die Sage erzählt, es sei der Teufel gewesen, und die Dorfleute hätten ihn überlistet, indem sie zuerst eine Ziege hinüberschickten. Die Steinbrücke, über die man heute gehen kann, stammt von 1830. Auf der Südseite führt die Tremola in 24 Kehren über Granitpflaster zum Pass, um 1830 vollendet. 1882 wurde der Eisenbahntunnel eröffnet, 1980 der Strassentunnel. Die Passstrasse ist im Winter gesperrt.

## nufenen — Nufenen Pass
Used in tours: Alpine journey "Nufenen"

### Fact-check of the brief
- "At about 2,477 metres" — CORRECTED — 2,478 m (HLS, alpen-paesse.ch, Wikipedia). — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/de/articles/008803/2013-02-28/ ; https://alpen-paesse.ch/en/alpenpaesse/nufenenpass/
- "among Switzerland's highest driveable pass roads" — CONFIRMED — the Umbrail (about 2,501 m) is higher but lies on the Swiss-Italian border; the Nufenen is the highest pass lying wholly in Switzerland. The "wholly in Switzerland" wording comes from a MySwitzerland route description and a cycling guide seen in search results; I could not open the MySwitzerland page, so the wording is single-sourced. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nufenen_Pass ; https://cycloclimbing.com/alpspass1700paved/14nufenen.html ; https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-ie/experiences/route/stgotthardvierpaessebike-8401

### Sourced facts

### Story
EN title: Nufenen, a road only since 1969
EN body: At 2,478 metres the Nufenen Pass links Ulrichen in the Valais with Airolo in Ticino. It is the highest road pass that lies entirely in Switzerland. The Umbrail is higher, but it sits on the Italian border. For centuries only a footpath crossed here, used to drive cattle and carry cheese. A road for cars opened in 1969. Up on the pass there is open ground, wind and a long view into both valleys. The road is usually open from late May to late October, depending on snow.
DE title: Der Nufenen, eine Strasse erst seit 1969
DE body: Mit 2478 Metern verbindet der Nufenenpass Ulrichen im Wallis mit Airolo im Tessin. Es ist der höchste Strassenpass, der ganz in der Schweiz liegt. Der Umbrail ist höher, liegt aber an der Grenze zu Italien. Jahrhundertelang führte hier nur ein Fussweg hinüber, auf dem man Vieh trieb und Käse trug. Eine Strasse für Autos wurde 1969 eröffnet. Oben gibt es offenes Gelände, Wind und einen weiten Blick in beide Täler. Die Strasse ist meist von Ende Mai bis Ende Oktober offen, je nach Schnee. Das Wetter ändert sich hier oben schnell.

## brienzer-rothorn — Brienzer Rothorn (cable car from Sörenberg, steam railway to Brienz, Giessbach)
Used in tours: Alpine journey "Brienzer Rothorn"

### Fact-check of the brief
- "ascend Brienzer Rothorn by cableway from Sörenberg" — CONFIRMED, wording note — it is an 80-person aerial tramway (Luftseilbahn), opened 2023 and replacing a 1971 tramway; not a gondola. — https://www.bergbahnen.org/en/database/ch/lu/s%25c3%25b6renberg/
- "descend towards Brienz on a cog railway whose historic steam services" — CONFIRMED with a caveat — the Brienz Rothorn Bahn opened 16 June 1892, built in 16 months by about 700 workers, mostly Italian, for CHF 2.2 million. Trains are pushed by steam locomotives, but the operator also uses diesel locomotives "at high demand or technical failure", so steam is not guaranteed on a given train. — https://www.brienz-rothorn-bahn.ch/bahngeschichte/ ; https://www.brienz-rothorn-bahn.ch/fahrplan-preise/
- "From Brienz, a lake boat can continue towards Giessbach Falls, with Thun as a possible extension" — CONFIRMED for Giessbach (BLS Brienzersee boats call at Giessbach; about 14 min from Brienz). Thun is on a different lake (Thunersee), reached via Interlaken; BLS runs both. — https://www.bls-schiff.ch/sites/default/files/2025-12/fahrplan_bls_schifffahrt_2026.pdf
- "operations are seasonal" — CONFIRMED, dates below.
- "Two days, or fewer stops, allow each chapter to breathe" — judgement; one-day version below keeps the cable car, the summit and the steam train, and drops other stops.

### Sourced facts
- Opened 16 June 1892; concession applied for 15 Oct 1889; operations suspended in the First World War and not electrified; restarted 1931; 2024 storm damage on about 2.2 km of track ended the 2024 season early — https://www.brienz-rothorn-bahn.ch/bahngeschichte/
- "Historic steam cog railway", about 60 minutes Brienz to Rothorn Kulm, over 2,200 m altitude, usually about 10 degrees colder than the valley — https://www.brienz-rothorn-bahn.ch/fahrplan-preise/
- Operator says the summit view reaches 693 peaks in good visibility (marketing claim, attribute it) — https://www.brienz-rothorn-bahn.ch/fahrplan-preise/
- Track length 7.6 km — https://www.interlaken.swiss/erlebnisse/poi/brienz-rothorn-bahn
- Smoke, steam and soot can enter carriages in tunnels; noisy locomotives; thinner air above 2,200 m — https://www.brienz-rothorn-bahn.ch/fahrplan-preise/
- Summit restaurant (Berghaus Rothorn Kulm / Sörenberg side restaurant) listed 4 June to 1 Nov 2026, 08:30-17:00 — https://www.soerenberg.ch/fileadmin/Mediendatenbank/Soerenberg/PDF/Bergbahnen/Broschueren___Prospekte/2026_Faltkarte_Uebersichtsplan_Rothorn.pdf (via search summary; PDF too large to open)
