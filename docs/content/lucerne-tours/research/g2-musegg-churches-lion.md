## musegg-zyt — Musegg Wall and Zyt Tower
Used in tours: 2 (Time, history and loyalty)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Musegg Wall and its nine towers" — CONFIRMED — nine of the original 30 towers survive — https://www.museggmauer.ch and https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/musegg-wall
- "the clock in Zyt Tower, dating from 1535" — CONFIRMED, with context — the clock mechanism bears the date 1535; Hansen Luter, a locksmith and clockmaker from Waldshut, built it as a replacement with the same layout as the first clock, which Heinrich Halder of Basel delivered in 1385 and which moved into the tower in 1403 — https://www.museggmauer.ch/zytturm and https://turmuhrenluzern.ch/en/project/zeitturm-uhr/
- "strikes the hour one minute before the city's other clocks" — CONFIRMED, wording adjusted — sources say it strikes one minute before the church clocks (MUS) or before the town-hall clock (a travel guide); the brief's "other clocks" is a fair summary. The privilege is called the "privilege of first stroke" — https://www.museggmauer.ch/zytturm and https://turmuhrenluzern.ch/en/project/zeitturm-uhr/
- "Some storytellers interpret the early chime as a symbol of civic authority; not a proven historical explanation" — CONFIRMED — the clock operator's site offers two explanations and proves neither: (1) when Lucerne put up a second public clock, the older one was allowed to strike first; (2) a political reading, that the city struck before the church bells to show independence — https://turmuhrenluzern.ch/en/project/zeitturm-uhr/
- "tower access is seasonal and involves stairs" — CONFIRMED (seasonal), stairs UNVERIFIED as to count — season 1 April to 1 November 2026; MYS says the wall walk is narrow with stairs and the site is not step-free. No source gives a stair count; I found none — https://www.museggmauer.ch and https://www.myswitzerland.com/de/erlebnisse/museggtuerme-mit-hof-hinter-musegg/
- Discrepancies the brief does not touch, noted for the writer: wall length 800 m (LZ) vs about 870 m (MUS); Zyt Tower built 1403 (MUS, and 36 m per a press source) vs "erected 1442" and 31 m (LZ). The story avoids both numbers except "about 800 metres". The 1442 date may refer to the roof; unconfirmed.

### Sourced facts
- Wall of about 800 m (LZ) or about 870 m, 9 m high, 1.5 m wide (MUS); called the longest intact city wall in Switzerland — https://www.museggmauer.ch
- Fortification of the hill began in the 13th century; of the original 30 towers, 9 survive — https://www.museggmauer.ch
- LZ dates the wall to around 1400, after the Battle of Sempach — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/musegg-wall
- Towers open to the public: Männliturm, Zytturm, Schirmerturm, and the lower part of the Wachtturm; wall walk and towers free, daily 08:00 to 19:00 — https://www.museggmauer.ch
- Zytturm open to the public since 1951; since 2012 it holds a collection of regional tower clocks, including its own, which can be seen running — https://www.museggmauer.ch/zytturm
- The Zytturm clock is described as the oldest tower clock in central Switzerland; MYS recommends being at the top shortly before the full hour — https://www.myswitzerland.com/de/erlebnisse/museggtuerme-mit-hof-hinter-musegg/
- Halder's 1385 operating instructions are the oldest known description of how a medieval clock worked and are held in the Lucerne state archives — https://turmuhrenluzern.ch/en/project/zeitturm-uhr/
- Since 1535 the city's clockmaker (Zytrichter) winds the clock by hand every day; a 9 m pendulum fitted in 1842 swings every three seconds — https://turmuhrenluzern.ch/en/project/zeitturm-uhr/
- Restoration of towers and wall ran 2007 to 2015 — https://www.museggmauer.ch

### Place data
- Coordinates [lon, lat]: [8.30448, 47.05435] (Zytturm, OSM via Nominatim: https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Zytturm+Luzern&format=json). LZ gives the wall as [8.30561, 47.05475]. The MYS map link [8.30935, 47.05117] points to the wrong spot; do not use it.

- 2026 access: open now (today is 2026-10-15). The season ends 1 November 2026. LZ says the wall and towers close from the beginning of November to the end of March. The next season starts 1 April; MUS gives 2026 dates only, so 1 April 2027 is an inference to confirm — https://www.museggmauer.ch and https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/museggmauer
- Step-free access / stairs: not wheelchair accessible; the wall walk is narrow with stairs; the Männliturm is not connected to the wall walk (Lonely Planet via search; secondary). No stair count found — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/museggmauer
- Time to allow: 15 to 20 minutes for the Zytturm visit (MYS); allow 30 to 45 if you walk the wall.

### Story
EN title: A Wall and a Clock That Strikes Early
EN body: Above the Old Town, the Musegg Wall runs for about 800 metres along the ridge. Lucerne began fortifying the hill in the 13th century, and nine of the original thirty towers still stand. In the Zyt Tower, a clock mechanism dated 1535 strikes each hour one minute before the church clocks. Why is not recorded for certain. One explanation is that the city wanted its public clock to speak first, as a sign of independence from the church bells. Treat that as a story, not proven history. Stand by the tower just before the hour and wait for it.

Warum, ist nicht sicher überliefert. Eine Erklärung lautet, die Stadt wollte ihre öffentliche Uhr zuerst sprechen lassen, als Zeichen der Unabhängigkeit von den Kirchenglocken. ## hofkirche — Hofkirche St. Leodegar
Used in tours: 2 (Time, history and loyalty)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Its twin towers recall an earlier Gothic church" — CORRECTED — the two towers survived the fire of 1633 and still show Romanesque elements (LZ). One page of de.wikipedia calls them Gothic in one passage and dates the two-tower complex to the second half of the 12th century, Romanesque, in another, so "Gothic" is not settled; "earlier church" is right — https://www.luzern.com/de/stadt/sehenswuerdigkeiten/top-sehenswuerdigkeiten/hofkirche and https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leodegar_im_Hof
- "much of the present structure arose from seventeenth-century reconstruction" — CONFIRMED — fire on Easter Sunday evening, 27 March 1633, burned the church to its foundations; first service in the rebuilt church at Easter 1638 (consecration date not confirmed; sources give 1639, 1644 or 1645) — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leodegar_im_Hof and https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/articles/012236 (caption confirms 27 March 1633)
- "Closer to the lake" — CONFIRMED — it stands near the lake end of the Old Town, about 900 m from the Jesuit Church; calculated from the coordinates below.
- Not in the brief: the Hofkirche is not the Dance of Death location; that is not this group's task.

### Sourced facts
- A small monastery was founded on the site around 735/736; by about 1135 it was a provostry of Murbach Abbey; 1291 sold to the Habsburgs; 1455/56 it became a collegiate chapter of canons — https://www.luzern.com/de/stadt/sehenswuerdigkeiten/top-sehenswuerdigkeiten/hofkirche (HLS dates differ slightly: 1456, and 1479 Lucerne bought the lordship rights for 2500 gulden — https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/articles/012236)
- Fire of 27 March 1633, started when a roofer was sent to shoot jackdaws nesting on the church towers — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leodegar_im_Hof (also a Luzerner Zeitung snippet: https://www.luzernerzeitung.ch/zentralschweiz/luzern/luzern-die-geheimnisvolle-seite-der-luzerner-hofkirche-ld.95365; not opened, search snippet only)
- Only the two towers survived; they still show Romanesque elements — https://www.luzern.com/de/stadt/sehenswuerdigkeiten/top-sehenswuerdigkeiten/hofkirche
- LZ calls it the most important Renaissance church building in Switzerland; the high altar is of black Unterwalden marble — same page.
- Each tower is 69 m high on a square base of about 9 m — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leodegar_im_Hof
- West front combines older towers, a Renaissance middle section and a Baroque gable (wiki says "Gothic towers"; see CORRECTED above) — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leodegar_im_Hof
- The great organ dates from the 1640s. Stop and pipe counts differ between sources (LZ: 111 stops, 7,374 pipes; de.wikipedia: 84 stops, 5,949 pipes), so the story gives no numbers — https://www.luzern.com/de/stadt/sehenswuerdigkeiten/top-sehenswuerdigkeiten/hofkirche
- The treasury is visited by guided tour only — same page.

### Place data
- Coordinates [lon, lat]: [8.31406, 47.05567] (de.wikipedia 47°3′20.4″N 8°18′50.6″E) — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leodegar_im_Hof
- Address: St.-Leodegar-Strasse 6, 6006 Luzern (parish office address; no separate church address on the parish page) — https://www.kathluzern.ch/meine-kirche/pfarreien-standorte/st-leodegar-im-hof. Stand in the forecourt at the west front, looking up at the two towers.
- 2026 access: church opening hours not confirmed. LZ and the parish page give none; Tripadvisor gives daily 07:00 to 19:00 (unofficial). Not a seasonal site, but services and concerts restrict visits. Treasury by guided tour only.
- Step-free access / stairs: UNVERIFIED; no source found.
- Time to allow: 15 to 20 minutes.

### Story
EN title: A Church Rebuilt Around Two Old Towers
EN body: A small monastery stood here as early as the 8th century. On Easter Sunday evening, 27 March 1633, a roofer was sent to shoot jackdaws nesting in the church towers. The shot started a fire that burned the church to its foundations. Only the two towers survived, and they still show Romanesque elements. The church was rebuilt within a few years, and the first service took place at Easter 1638. Luzern Tourismus calls the result the most important Renaissance church building in Switzerland. Look at the west front: older towers, a Renaissance middle and a Baroque gable.
DE title: Eine Kirche, neu gebaut um zwei alte Türme
DE body: Schon im 8. Jahrhundert stand hier ein kleines Kloster. Am Abend des Ostersonntags, dem 27. März 1633, sollte ein Dachdecker Dohlen abschiessen, die auf den Kirchtürmen nisteten. Der Schuss löste einen Brand aus, der die Kirche bis auf die Grundmauern zerstörte. Nur die beiden Türme blieben stehen, sie zeigen noch romanische Bauelemente. Die Kirche wurde in wenigen Jahren wieder aufgebaut, an Ostern 1638 fand der erste Gottesdienst statt. Luzern Tourismus nennt sie den bedeutendsten Renaissance-Kirchenbau der Schweiz. Achten Sie an der Westfassade auf die älteren Türme, den Renaissance-Mittelteil und den Barockgiebel.

## lion-monument — Lion Monument (Löwendenkmal)
Used in tours: 2 (Time, history and loyalty), 3 (The city's quieter lives), 4 (Lucerne at your own pace). Tour 1 may add it with two hours (brief).

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Completed in 1821" — CONFIRMED — carved 1820 to 1821 and inaugurated on 10 August 1821, the 29th anniversary of the Tuileries attack — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Monument and https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/loewendenkmal-luzern-schweizer-sterben-fuer-einen-koenig-darum-weint-der-luzerner-loewe
- "carved directly into the rock" — CONFIRMED — carved into a sandstone cliff face, about 10 by 6 m — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/lion-monument and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Monument
- "honours Swiss Guards who died during the attack on the Tuileries Palace in Paris in 1792" — CONFIRMED — storming of the Tuileries, 10 August 1792 — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/lion-monument
- "Its body collapses and its face is exhausted" — CONFIRMED as description; a spear is lodged in the lion's side and a paw rests on a shield with the French lilies (seen in the photo and in the Wikipedia article).
- Missing from the brief: Bertel Thorvaldsen designed it; Lukas Ahorn carved it. Urs Pankraz Eggenschwiler began the carving and died after a fall — https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/loewendenkmal-luzern-schweizer-sterben-fuer-einen-koenig-darum-weint-der-luzerner-loewe
- Number of dead: the inscription gives DCCLX (760) dead and CCCL (350) survivors. SRF quotes historian Jürg Stadelmann, who estimates 300 to 350 dead, against the monument's claim of over 700. Treat numbers as contested — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Monument and the SRF page above.
- "Old Swiss House near Löwenplatz" — CONFIRMED — see old-swiss-house.

### Sourced facts
- Commissioned by Karl Pfyffer von Altishofen, a Guard officer on leave in Lucerne in 1792 — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/lion-monument
- Designed by Bertel Thorvaldsen; Ahorn completed the carving in 1820 and 1821 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Monument
- Inscription "Helvetiorum fidei ac virtuti" (to the loyalty and bravery of the Swiss); it names only the fallen officers — https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/loewendenkmal-luzern-schweizer-sterben-fuer-einen-koenig-darum-weint-der-luzerner-loewe
- Historian Stadelmann calls it a propaganda site for the mercenary trade and suggests it also stands for about 500 years of Swiss mercenary service — same SRF page.
- Under monument protection since 2006; about 1.4 million visitors a year; Mark Twain called it "the most mournful and moving piece of stone in the world" (A Tramp Abroad, 1880) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Monument
- Information panels: LZ says ten boards in German and English with a QR code for French and Italian; SRF in 2021 says seven new panels. Not used in the story — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/lion-monument

### Place data
- Coordinates [lon, lat]: [8.31053, 47.05831] — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/loewendenkmal
- Address: Denkmalstrasse 4, 6002 Luzern. Stand on the path by the pond facing the cliff; the Löwenplatz bus stop is the nearest (lines 1 and 19 from the station).
- 2026 access: free, no opening hours stated; an outdoor park monument, open year-round (hours not confirmed) — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/lion-monument
- Step-free access / stairs: wheelchair accessible per LZ — same page.
- Time to allow: 20 to 30 minutes (brief); 15 minutes is enough for the monument alone.

### Story
EN title: A Dying Lion Carved Into the Rock
EN body: The lion lies in a hollow cut into a sandstone cliff, about 10 metres long and 6 metres high. Bertel Thorvaldsen designed it, and the stonemason Lukas Ahorn carved it in 1820 and 1821. It remembers the Swiss Guards killed defending King Louis XVI at the Tuileries Palace in Paris on 10 August 1792. A spear is lodged in the lion's side, and a paw rests on a shield with the French lilies. The inscription, "Helvetiorum fidei ac virtuti", means "to the loyalty and bravery of the Swiss". It names 760 dead. Some historians think the real toll was lower. Take a quiet moment.
DE title: Der sterbende Löwe im Fels
DE body: Der Löwe liegt in einer Nische einer Sandsteinwand, rund 10 Meter lang und 6 Meter hoch. Bertel Thorvaldsen entwarf ihn, der Steinmetz Lukas Ahorn hieb ihn 1820 und 1821 aus dem Fels. Er erinnert an die Schweizergardisten, die am 10. August 1792 beim Sturm auf die Tuilerien in Paris König Ludwig XVI. verteidigten und fielen. Im Körper des Löwen steckt ein Speer, eine Pranke ruht auf einem Schild mit den französischen Lilien. Die Inschrift «Helvetiorum fidei ac virtuti» bedeutet «der Treue und Tapferkeit der Schweizer». Sie nennt 760 Tote. Manche Historiker schätzen die Zahl tiefer. Nehmen Sie sich einen ruhigen Moment.

## old-swiss-house — Old Swiss House (Löwenplatz 4)
Used in tours: 2 (Time, history and loyalty; optional meal)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Old Swiss House near Löwenplatz is convenient for a traditional meal" — CONFIRMED with detail — the address is Löwenplatz 4, about 130 m from the Lion Monument (calculated from the coordinates). The cooking is Swiss with French influence (veal escalope fried at the table), so "traditional" holds loosely — https://www.luzern.com/en/gastro/old-swiss-house
- The brief gives no building history. New facts: a half-timbered house (Riegelhaus) permitted in December 1858, a restaurant since 1931 — https://www.oldswisshouse.ch/geschichte
- LZ and the restaurant agree on 1858 and 1931. Search results also mention 1859, which is the year the taxidermy museum opened in the house, not the building year.

### Sourced facts
- 17 December 1858: the city council gave Samuel Stauffer, a taxidermist, a permit to build a half-timbered house on his property "Weymatte", near the 1821 Lion Monument. He opened a museum of stuffed Alpine animals in October 1859 — https://www.oldswisshouse.ch/geschichte
- 1888: Caspar Hirsbrunner bought it, used it as a residence and photography studio — same page.
- 1931: Wilhelm Buholzer owned it; the Gasthaus licence was transferred from Hotel Mohren; Ida Buholzer ran the kitchen — same page.
- General Guisan was a frequent guest and, according to the restaurant, held secret meetings in the Röselizimmer during the war years — same page.
- Antiques from the 16th century in the rooms; veal escalope fried at the table; 15 Gault Millau points (LZ; date of the rating unknown) — https://www.luzern.com/en/gastro/old-swiss-house
- Wiener Schnitzel at the table and a Mouton Rothschild wine collection are listed on the restaurant site — https://www.oldswisshouse.ch (not used in the story).
- A 1951 story (August A. Busch marrying Trudy Buholzer in 1952) appears in one secondary search result only; not used.

### Place data
- Coordinates [lon, lat]: [8.31126, 47.05728] — https://www.luzern.com/en/gastro/old-swiss-house
- Address: Löwenplatz 4, 6004 Luzern. Phone +41 41 410 61 71.
- 2026 access: a restaurant, not a sight. Per the restaurant's site: Tuesday to Saturday 11:30 to 14:30 (warm kitchen until 13:30) and 18:00 to 23:30 (warm kitchen until 21:00); closed Sunday and Monday. Reservations online or by phone — https://www.oldswisshouse.ch. Hours not checked for 2026 holidays.
- Step-free access: UNVERIFIED; no source.
- Time to allow: 60 to 90 minutes for a meal; 5 minutes if only looking at the house.

### Story
EN title: A Half-Timbered House Turned Restaurant
EN body: Not every building near the Lion Monument is a monument. In December 1858 the city council allowed Samuel Stauffer, a taxidermist, to build a half-timbered house here. In October 1859 he opened a museum of stuffed Alpine animals in it. Later owners used it as a home and photo studio. Since 1931 it has been a restaurant, with antiques in its rooms. According to the restaurant, General Henri Guisan held secret meetings in its Röseli room during the Second World War. The kitchen is known today for veal escalope fried at the table.
DE title: Ein Riegelhaus wird zum Restaurant
DE body: Nicht jedes Haus beim Löwendenkmal ist ein Denkmal. Im Dezember 1858 bewilligte der Luzerner Stadtrat dem Tierpräparator Samuel Stauffer ein Riegelhaus an dieser Stelle. Im Oktober 1859 eröffnete er darin ein Museum mit ausgestopften Alpentieren. Spätere Besitzer nutzten das Haus als Wohnhaus und Fotoatelier. Seit 1931 ist es ein Restaurant mit Antiquitäten in den Stuben, die zum Teil aus dem 16. Jahrhundert stammen. Nach Angaben des Hauses hielt General Henri Guisan im Zweiten Weltkrieg im Röselizimmer geheime Besprechungen ab. Bekannt ist die Küche heute für das Kalbsschnitzel, das am Tisch gebraten wird.

## jesuit-church — Jesuit Church (Jesuitenkirche St. Franz Xaver)

### Sourced facts
- Foundation stone 3 December 1666, the feast day of St Francis Xavier; consecrated 29 August 1677 by the papal nuncio Edoardo Cibo — https://jesuitenkirche-luzern.ch/geschichte/
- Francis Xavier (1506 to 1552) was canonised in 1622; Lucerne's council chose him as city patron in 1654 — same page.
- Jesuit order dissolved 1773; the school continued and became the cantonal school, which moved out in 1966 — same page.
- Designer uncertain; candidates include Michael Beer, Michael Thumb and the Jesuits Heinrich Mayer and Christoph Vogler — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuitenkirche_(Luzern)
- Owned by the canton and made available to the Catholic Church; listed as nationally significant (category A) — same page.
- Rococo stucco redone in 1749/50 (search-snippet only, source not opened; not used in the story) — UNVERIFIED.
- Nave ceiling fresco by the Torricelli brothers shows the saint in a carriage drawn by an elephant, dromedary, horse and cheetah (de.wikipedia only; not used in the story) — UNVERIFIED.
- Used for concerts and organ teaching because of its acoustics — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/jesuit-church
