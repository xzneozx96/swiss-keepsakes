## chapel-bridge — Chapel Bridge and Water Tower (Kapellbrücke, Wasserturm)
Used in tours: 1 (Lucerne in 90 minutes), 4 (Lucerne at your own pace)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "the covered timber bridge ... was once part of the town defences" — CONFIRMED — the bridge and tower formed part of the lake-side fortifications; the bridge is named after St Peter's Chapel — https://www.kapellbruecke.com/en/chapel-bridge/ and https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/lucernes-landmarks-the-chapel-bridge-and-its-water-tower
- "The triangular paintings form a history book in images, sponsored by prominent citizens" — CONFIRMED with nuance — the picture programme was designed by town clerk Renward Cysat (finalised 1611); because the city could not pay, wealthy citizens were asked to donate panels and could add their family arms (art historian Heinz Horat). Painter of the cycle: Hans Heinrich Wägmann. Themes: Swiss history and the legends of the patron saints Leodegar and Maurice — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/lucernes-landmarks-the-chapel-bridge-and-its-water-tower ; Wägmann: https://hls-dhs-dss.ch/articles/019165 ; donors *(snippet from luzern.com DE page, not re-read)*: https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/kapellbruecke
- "On the night of 18 August 1993, fire devastated much of the bridge and its paintings" — CONFIRMED — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/lucernes-landmarks-the-chapel-bridge-and-its-water-tower
- "Lucerne rebuilt the structure in eight months and reopened it in April 1994" — CONFIRMED — "a record eight months"; reopened 14 April 1994 — same luzern.com page; date also in https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapellbrücke
- "Missing paintings remind us that rebuilding timber is not the same as restoring every memory" — CONFIRMED (not every lost panel was replaced). Wikipedia DE: 78 of 111 paintings destroyed, remnants of 47 recovered, 30 restored by 1998, 25 replacement panels from the 1830s hung in the central section — https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapellbrücke [secondary]
- "The octagonal Water Tower is older than the bridge" — CONFIRMED — tower c. 1290-1300; bridge: first documented 1367, tree-ring dating gives no earlier than 1356 — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/lucernes-landmarks-the-chapel-bridge-and-its-water-tower and https://www.kapellbruecke.com/en/chapel-bridge/
- "has served as an archive, treasury and prison" — CONFIRMED — lookout, fortification, archive from the 14th century (archives moved out 1919), cantonal treasury, war-plunder store, prison until late 18th century — luzern.com page above.
- Not in the brief but found: painting counts conflict across sources. Original total 158 (luzern.com, KGS snippet) or 111 (Wikipedia DE, kapellbruecke.com); destroyed 78, 85 or 86. Only "about two-thirds" is consistent, so the story uses that. Cause of fire: kapellbruecke.com says "likely a carelessly discarded cigarette", another source says unknown, so it is left out. Build date and painting dates (c. 1607 vs 1614-1624) also differ and are left out.

### Sourced facts
- Water Tower is 34.5 m high, octagonal, circumference 39 m (inyourpocket via search snippet) — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/lucernes-landmarks-the-chapel-bridge-and-its-water-tower
- Bridge today is about 203-205 m long (205 m per kapellbruecke.com; 202.9 m per Wikipedia DE); originally 279 m, shortened in 1833, 1835/36, 1838 and 1898 — https://www.kapellbruecke.com/en/chapel-bridge/
- Fire broke out in the night of 18 August 1993; the Water Tower survived — luzern.com page above.
- The paintings were photographed professionally two years before the fire, which let art historians study the whole cycle (Horat, 2-volume study 2015) *(snippet)* — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/kapellbruecke
- Bridge links the south bank (Theatre) with St Peter's Chapel on Rathausquai — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/chapel-bridge
- Today an armoury above the dungeon displays weapons of the Artillery Association — luzern.com page above.

### Place data
- Coordinates [lon, lat]: [8.30724, 47.05134] (map link on luzern.com bridge page; Water Tower per OSM 8.30729, 47.05128) — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/chapel-bridge
- Address / where to stand: Kapellbrücke, 6002 Luzern. Start at the Rathausquai (north) end by St Peter's Chapel or at the Bahnhofstrasse end; the Water Tower is mid-bridge.
- 2026 access: bridge is a public walkway, no opening hours listed on luzern.com (open now). Water Tower interior: no public hours found; one guide says it is seldom accessible. Do not promise a tower visit. — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/chapel-bridge ; https://www.inyourpocket.com/lucerne/Wasserturm_125660v
- Step-free access / stairs: listed as wheelchair accessible on luzern.com — https://www.luzern.com/en/poi/chapel-bridge . Tower stairs: no information.
- Time to allow: 15 minutes.

## kornmarkt — Kornmarkt and the Town Hall (Rathaus)
Used in tours: 1 (Lucerne in 90 minutes), 4 (Lucerne at your own pace)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Fountains, painted façades and historic windows invite the walker to look up" — CONFIRMED in general for the three squares (luzern.com says Old Town squares have fresco-decorated houses); not specific to Kornmarkt — https://www.luzern.com/de/stadt/sehenswuerdigkeiten/plaetze-parks/malerische-altstadtplaetze
- "the Town Hall at Kornmarkt speaks of civic authority and merchant life" — CONFIRMED — city built a public trading hall (Kaufhaus) at Kornmarkt around 1370, used as a granary and later converted to the town hall; the current Rathaus was built 1602-1606 and the council chamber is on the first floor — https://luzern.com/en/poi/kornmarkt-and-hirschenplatz and https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/town-hall-on-kornmarkt . Note the sources do not make clear whether the 1370 hall and the 1602-06 building are the same structure, so the story keeps them as separate sentences.
- "Decorated façades were not simply pretty: they signalled family, wealth, belief and standing" — UNVERIFIED as a statement about these squares. It is plausible interpretation, and it is the brief's storytelling rather than a sourced fact. Not used in the story.
- "Imagine a cloth merchant crossing the square" — storytelling device, not a factual claim. Not used.
- "angled Town Hall view" (photo-stop tip) — no source needed; kept as a viewing suggestion.

### Sourced facts
- Around 1370 the city built a public trading house on the Kornmarkt, also used as a Kornhaus (granary) and later converted into the Rathaus — https://luzern.com/en/poi/kornmarkt-and-hirschenplatz
- Town Hall built 1602-1606 by the Italian architect and builder Anton Isenmann, Italian Renaissance; low, broad roof in a farmhouse style (luzern.com EN says Bernese farmhouse, another source says Lucerne farmhouse; story says "farmhouse") that shelters the building from wind and weather — https://www.luzern.com/en/the-city/sights/top-sights/town-hall-on-kornmarkt
- Ground floor (Kornschütte) was a trading hall and now hosts exhibitions and concerts — same page.
- First-floor council chamber: coffered ceiling, two large paintings by Melchior Wyrsch and Josef Reinhart — same page.
- Empire-style Tagsatzung Room (Josef Singer) is used for civil weddings — same page.
- Medieval family tower holds Renward Cysat's Old Chancellery on the top floor — same page.
- Dovecote in the attic since 2003; groups of 10 or more can visit — same page.
- Weekly market Tuesdays and Saturdays 06:00-13:00 on Rathausquai, along the Reuss arcades, Jesuitenplatz and part of Bahnhofstrasse — same page.

## weinmarkt — Weinmarkt, fountain and painted facades
Used in tours: 1 (Lucerne in 90 minutes), 3 (The city's quieter lives), 4 (Lucerne at your own pace)

### Fact-check of the brief
- "Weinmarkt recalls the world of trade" — CONFIRMED — fish market until the mid-16th century; a two-storey wooden market hall (meat, bread, leather) stood here until it was demolished in 1841, giving the square its present form — https://www.luzern.com/de/stadt/sehenswuerdigkeiten/plaetze-parks/malerische-altstadtplaetze
- "Fountains, painted façades ... invite the walker to look up" — CONFIRMED — Weinmarktbrunnen of 1481 by Konrad Lux (about ten years' work); historic houses with frescoes; one facade painting by Seraphin Weingartner on the former Alte Apotheke — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/weinmarkt
- Not in the brief but a real hook: the Lucerners swore the alliance with Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden on the Weinmarkt in 1332 — luzern.com page above. The 1332 date for Lucerne joining the Confederation is standard Swiss history; the claim that the oath was sworn on this square is luzern.com's.
- The brief says "fountain" without saying that the fountain in place is not the 1481 original. CORRECTED/added: the original is in the Historisches Museum, Pfistergasse; the current stock was carved by Leopold Häfliger from shell limestone; the hexagonal basin was replaced by an octagonal one in the 16th century — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/weinmarkt
- "Decorated façades signalled family, wealth, belief and standing" — UNVERIFIED (interpretation). Not used.
- A luzern.com photo caption places the Fritschibrunnen on the Weinmarkt, but the main text places it on the Kapellplatz. I used the text. The fountain on the Weinmarkt is the Weinmarktbrunnen (also called the St Maurice fountain by tour guides *(snippet, secondary)*).

### Sourced facts
- Oath of 1332 on this square — https://www.luzern.com/de/poi/weinmarkt
- Fish market until mid-16th century; present square form since 1841 — same page.
- Weinmarktbrunnen 1481, Konrad Lux, about ten years' work; original now in the Historisches Museum — same page.
- Facade painting by Seraphin Weingartner on the former Alte Apotheke — same page (image caption, painting date not given).
- A travel blog names the facade of Haus zur Sonne as "Wedding at Cana" by Eduard Renggli, 1928 *(snippet, unverified, not used)* — https://travelfeed.com/@delishtreats/switzerland-travel-series-part-6-lucerne
